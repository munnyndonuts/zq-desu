Full path: The complete Jalup series (Beginner → Champion) spans 7,000 cards and aims for ~2,500+ kanji and ~10,000 vocabulary. WaniKani Level 3: At your current pace, you'll hit Level 10 (300 kanji) in roughly 7 weeks, and Level 13 (~480 kanji, ~jouyou kanji for N3) in about 10 weeks. This aligns well with starting Jalup Beginner around the time you hit Level 4.

Lesson schedule: 10–15 lessons on weekdays, reviews only on weekends. This is a solid pace — at ~12.5 lessons/day average, you'll clear each level's ~50–60 lessons in about a week. Keep an eye on your review pile; if it exceeds ~150/day, reduce new lessons temporarily.

Bunpro overlap: Jalup Beginner covers the same N5–N4 grammar as Bunpro. Once you start Jalup Beginner (Phase 2), consider pausing Bunpro grammar to avoid redundancy — or keep it as a supplementary review at reduced pace (1 pt/5 vocab per day → 1 pt every 2 days). For Bunpro vocab, the N5 set overlaps heavily with Kaishi, so you can safely deprioritize it.
🧭 Continuing to Jalup Advanced, Expert & Hero: Is It Advisable?
Jalup Advanced (Deck 3)
1,000 cards · N3–N2
Verdict: Yes, advisable. Expands vocabulary to ~3,000 words total. By this point, J-J definitions feel natural. Advanced is reportedly easier than Intermediate because you're already comfortable with monolingual cards. Expected comprehension: Japanese TV 35%, Manga 40%, Novels 25%. ~2,000 kanji.
Jalup Expert (Deck 4)
1,000 cards · N2
Verdict: Yes, if you enjoy the structured approach. Brings total to ~4,000 sentences. More natural expressions, idiom patterns, and formal register. Expected comprehension: Japanese TV 60%, Manga 65%, Novels 50%. ~2,100 kanji. At this stage, immersion should overtake study time.
Jalup Hero (Deck 5)
1,000 cards · N2+
Verdict: Optional but beneficial. Completes the core 5,000-card series. By the end, you should handle most conversations, write long passages, and understand ~60% of TV/manga. This is roughly JLPT N2 equivalent. ~2,250 kanji total. Many learners stop here and go full immersion.
Jalup Master + Champion (Decks 6–7)
2,000 cards · N1
Verdict: Consider later. Themed around popular manga (One Piece, Dragon Ball, Death Note, Fullmetal Alchemist). Only available as Anki decks (not in the iOS app). Target: ~2,500+ kanji, 10,000+ vocab, near-fluent comprehension. Decide whether to continue or switch to pure sentence mining after Hero.
Overall recommendation: Yes, continuing through Advanced and Expert is advisable. Here's why:

1. Advanced is easier than Intermediate. The J-J learning curve flattens after Intermediate. If you can survive Intermediate, Advanced feels like a natural continuation, not a step up in difficulty (Reddit reviews).

2. Structured vocabulary expansion. Advanced+ adds ~3,000 more words in i+1 order. This is more efficient than random sentence mining at the N3–N2 level, where native content has too many unknowns per sentence.

3. Grammar is front-loaded. Most grammar is taught in Beginner. Advanced+ focuses on vocabulary, expressions, and reading speed — the exact skills you need for your goal of playing games and watching anime in Japanese.

4. The level guide confirms the path. Jalup's own proficiency scale places Advanced at ~1.5 years (Proficient), Expert/Hero at ~2 years (Advanced), and full completion at ~3 years (Work Capable) (Japanese Level Up).

When to stop and switch to mining: After completing Intermediate, you have enough foundation to start sentence mining from native content. Many learners do this instead of continuing to Advanced — both paths work. The optimal approach is a hybrid: continue Jalup Advanced at 5 cards/day while increasing immersion mining. By Expert/Hero, shift to 80% immersion / 20% SRS. After Hero, transition fully to sentence mining and native content.
🛠️ Recommended Resources
Jalup Beginner (Nihongo Lessons)
App/Anki · ~$50 or $5/mo
1,000 i+1 sentence cards covering all Genki I+II grammar. Available on iOS (Nihongo Lessons app) or as Anki decks via Jalup Discord. The direct bridge between Kaishi and Jalup Intermediate.
Tae Kim's Grammar Guide
Free · Grammar Reference
Standard free grammar reference. Covers N5–N4 (some N3). Use alongside Jalup Beginner when a grammar point needs more explanation — Jalup teaches inductively, so a reference is valuable.
Cure Dolly (YouTube)
Free · Grammar Video
"Organic Japanese" grammar video series. Explains the "why" behind particles and conjugations. Watch 1–2 videos/week alongside Jalup Beginner for deeper understanding.
AIAIJ Textbook
Textbook · ~$45
An Integrated Approach to Intermediate Japanese. The textbook Jalup Intermediate's grammar is based on. 7 chapters, ~150 N4–N3 grammar points. Study in Phase 3 before or alongside Jalup Intermediate.
Quartet I & II
Textbook · ~$45 each
Modern successor to AIAIJ. Quartet I covers N3, II covers N2. Consider if you prefer a newer textbook with better readings sourced from real books and articles.
Bunpro N3 Grammar
SRS · $3–5/mo
You're already using Bunpro. Their AIAIJ deck has 150 grammar items that directly mirror Jalup Intermediate. Study 1 point/day in Phase 3–4 for parallel reinforcement.
A Dictionary of Basic/Intermediate Grammar
Reference · ~$45 each
Most comprehensive English-language Japanese grammar reference. Basic = N5–N4, Intermediate = N3. Use as reference when grammar points feel unclear, not cover-to-cover.
Tadoku Graded Readers
Free + Paid · Reading
Free levels 0–7 at tadoku.org. Start Level 0–1 alongside Kaishi, Level 2–3 alongside Jalup Beginner, Level 4 before Jalup Intermediate. Essential for reading speed and pattern recognition.
Satori Reader
Subscription · ~$9/mo
N4–N3 Japanese stories with built-in grammar notes, furigana toggle, and audio. Perfect bridge between graded readers and native content. Start in Phase 3.
NHK News Web Easy
Free · Reading
Simplified news articles with furigana and audio. N4-level. 1 article/week starting Phase 2. Great for encountering N3 grammar in real-world context.
Crystal Hunters
Free/Paid · Manga
N4-level manga designed for learners. Controlled vocabulary and grammar. Good transition from graded readers to native manga.
Yomitan + J-J Dictionary
Free · Tool
Install 大辞林 (Daijirin) or 明鏡国語辞典 (Meikyo) in Yomitan. Start with J-E definitions, gradually switch to J-J. Critical prep for Jalup Intermediate's monolingual format.
🔑 Key Takeaways
Biggest Strength
Kaishi + Beginner = Complementary
Kaishi gives you the vocabulary Jalup Beginner skips. Jalup Beginner gives you the grammar Kaishi lacks. Running them in parallel is the optimal approach — no redundancy.
Biggest Win from Adding Beginner
Grammar Gap Closes
Jalup Beginner covers all N5–N4 grammar (~130 points) that Kaishi completely lacks. This means you can pause Bunpro and learn grammar inductively through sentences — more effective for building intuition.
Remaining Gap After Beginner
J-J Transition + N3 Grammar
After Kaishi + Beginner, you'll have N5–N4 grammar and ~1,700 vocab words. But Jalup Intermediate still requires: (1) ~150 N3 grammar points, (2) J-J dictionary skills, (3) ~1,000 kanji recognition. These need the Phase 3 prep work.
Biggest Bottleneck
Kanji Volume
You're at ~60 kanji (WaniKani Lv.2). Jalup Beginner assumes ~1,000. This is the longest ramp and needs dedicated focus. Prioritize WaniKani or add a kanji-specific deck.
Bottom line: Adding Jalup Beginner to your path is a smart move. It fills the grammar gap that Kaishi has, and it's the direct prerequisite for Jalup Intermediate. Start Jalup Beginner when you're ~75% through Kaishi (around ~1,125 cards done, ~375 remaining), run both in parallel for ~3 months, then do the Phase 3 J-J prep work before entering Jalup Intermediate. Expect ~7–8 months from now to be ready for Intermediate. Continuing to Advanced/Expert/Hero is advisable — see the full-path analysis above.
