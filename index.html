Key insight: Kaishi and Jalup Beginner are complementary, not redundant. Kaishi gives you the vocabulary foundation Jalup Beginner deliberately skips; Jalup Beginner gives you the grammar foundation Kaishi completely lacks. Running them in parallel (or overlapping) is the optimal approach.

Full path: The complete Jalup series (Beginner → Champion) spans 7,000 cards and aims for ~2,500+ kanji and ~10,000 vocabulary. Advanced/Expert/Hero are easier than Intermediate because by then you're comfortable with J-J definitions. Many learners switch to sentence mining after Intermediate — both paths are valid.

🧭 Continuing to Jalup Advanced, Expert & Hero: Is It Advisable?
Jalup Advanced (Deck 3)
1,000 cards · N3–N2
Verdict: Yes, advisable. Expands vocabulary to ~3,000 words total. By this point, J-J definitions feel natural. Advanced is reportedly easier than Intermediate because you're already comfortable with monolingual cards. Expected comprehension: Japanese TV 35%, Manga 40%, Novels 25%. ~2,000 kanji.
Jalup Expert (Deck 4)
1,000 cards · N2
Verdict: Yes, if you enjoy the structured approach. Brings total to ~4,000 sentences. More natural expressions, idiom patterns, and formal register. Expected comprehension: Japanese TV 60%, Manga 65%, Novels 50%. ~2,100 kanji. At this stage, immersion should overtake study time.
Jalup Hero (Deck 5)
1,000 cards · N2+
Verdict: Optional but beneficial. Completes the core 5,000-card series. By the end, you should handle most conversations, write long passages, and understand ~60% of TV/manga. This is roughly JLPT N2 equivalent. ~2,250 kanji total. Many learners stop here and go full immersion.
Jalup Master + Champion (Decks 6–7)
2,000 cards · N1
Verdict: Consider later. Themed around popular manga (One Piece, Dragon Ball, Death Note, Fullmetal Alchemist). Only available as Anki decks (not in the iOS app). Target: ~2,500+ kanji, 10,000+ vocab, near-fluent comprehension. Decide whether to continue or switch to pure sentence mining after Hero.
Overall recommendation: Yes, continuing through Advanced and Expert is advisable. Here's why:

1. Advanced is easier than Intermediate. The J-J learning curve flattens after Intermediate. If you can survive Intermediate, Advanced feels like a natural continuation, not a step up in difficulty (Reddit reviews).

2. Structured vocabulary expansion. Advanced+ adds ~3,000 more words in i+1 order. This is more efficient than random sentence mining at the N3–N2 level, where native content has too many unknowns per sentence.

3. Grammar is front-loaded. Most grammar is taught in Beginner. Advanced+ focuses on vocabulary, expressions, and reading speed — the exact skills you need for your goal of playing games and watching anime in Japanese.

4. The level guide confirms the path. Jalup's own proficiency scale places Advanced at ~1.5 years (Proficient), Expert/Hero at ~2 years (Advanced), and full completion at ~3 years (Work Capable) (Japanese Level Up).

When to stop and switch to mining: After completing Intermediate, you have enough foundation to start sentence mining from native content. Many learners do this instead of continuing to Advanced — both paths work. The optimal approach is a hybrid: continue Jalup Advanced at 5 cards/day while increasing immersion mining. By Expert/Hero, shift to 80% immersion / 20% SRS. After Hero, transition fully to sentence mining and native content.
🛠️ Recommended Resources
Tae Kim's Grammar Guide
Free · Grammar Reference
Standard free grammar reference. Covers N5–N4 (some N3). Use alongside Bunpro in Phase 1 when a grammar point needs more explanation — Bunpro teaches inductively, so a reference is valuable.
Cure Dolly (YouTube)
Free · Grammar Video
"Organic Japanese" grammar video series. Explains the "why" behind particles and conjugations. Watch 1–2 videos/week in Phase 1 alongside WaniKani and Bunpro for a deeper structural understanding of the language.
Tadoku Graded Readers
Free + Paid · Reading
Free levels 0–7 at tadoku.org. Start Level 0–1 in Phase 1 alongside Kaishi, Level 2–3 in Phase 2 alongside Jalup Beginner, Level 4+ before Jalup Intermediate in Phase 3. Essential for reading speed and pattern recognition throughout your study plan.
Satori Reader
Subscription · ~$9/mo
N4–N3 Japanese stories with built-in grammar notes, furigana toggle, and audio. Already in your Phase 1 daily reading rotation alongside Tadoku. Continues through Phase 2–3 as difficulty increases.
A Dictionary of Basic/Intermediate Grammar
Reference · ~$45 each
Most comprehensive English-language Japanese grammar reference. Basic covers N5–N4 (Phase 1–2), Intermediate covers N3 (Phase 3). Use as a lookup reference when grammar points feel unclear — not cover-to-cover study.
Jalup Beginner (Nihongo Lessons)
App (Nihongo Lessons) · ~$50 or $5/mo
1,000 i+1 sentence cards covering all Genki I+II grammar (N5–N4). Accessed through the Nihongo Lessons app (iOS). Start in Phase 2 when Kaishi is ~80–90% done. The direct bridge between Kaishi and Jalup Intermediate — fills the grammar gap Kaishi leaves.
Crystal Hunters
Free/Paid · Manga
N4-level manga designed for learners. Controlled vocabulary and grammar. Good transition in Phase 2 from graded readers to native manga before tackling Yotsuba& in Phase 3.
AIAIJ Textbook
Textbook · ~$45
An Integrated Approach to Intermediate Japanese. The textbook Jalup Intermediate's grammar is based on. 7 chapters, ~150 N4–N3 grammar points. Study in Phase 3 before or alongside Jalup Intermediate's J-J transition.
Quartet I & II
Textbook · ~$45 each
Modern successor to AIAIJ. Quartet I covers N3, II covers N2. Consider in Phase 3 if you prefer a newer textbook with better readings sourced from real books and articles.
Bunpro N3 Grammar
SRS · $3–5/mo
You're already using Bunpro. Their AIAIJ deck has 150 grammar items that directly mirror Jalup Intermediate. Study 1 point/day in Phase 3 for parallel reinforcement alongside your Jalup Intermediate cards.
NHK News Web Easy
Free · Reading
Simplified news articles with furigana and audio. N4-level. Start in Phase 3 alongside native manga and simple novels for real-world grammar exposure. Great for encountering N3 grammar in authentic context.
Yomitan + J-J Dictionary
Free · Tool
Install 大辞林 (Daijirin) or 明鏡国語辞典 (Meikyo) in Yomitan. Critical for Phase 3's Jalup Intermediate J-J transition. Set up J-J definitions before starting Jalup Intermediate so the monolingual format feels natural from day one.
